## Why it matters

Even a strong password can be stolen through no fault of yours, for example when a company that holds your details is hacked. Two-step verification adds a second lock to your account, so a password alone is not enough to get in. In this lesson you turn it on for your Google or Apple account.

## The idea

With two-step verification, getting into your account takes two steps instead of one:

1. **First step:** your password.
2. **Second step:** a confirmation that usually comes to your phone. It can be a 6-digit verification code in a text message, or a prompt where you tap "Yes" if it is you trying to sign in, and "No" if it is not.

So if a scammer learns your password, there is still a second lock that needs your phone.

Usually the second step is not asked every time, only when you sign in on a new device, for example.

The feature has different names: Google calls it "2-Step Verification", Apple calls it "two-factor authentication", and Saudi Arabia's National Cybersecurity Authority calls it "two-factor verification". You may also see "2FA" or "multi-factor authentication (MFA)". It is usually in your account's security settings.

## Step by step

These are the steps for a Google Account and an Apple Account, as their official help pages describe them. The date they were last checked is shown at the bottom of this lesson.

### Google Account (Android and Gmail)

1. Open your browser and go to `myaccount.google.com`, then sign in.
2. Tap **Security & sign-in**.
3. Under "How you sign in to Google", tap **2-Step Verification**, then turn it on.
4. Follow the steps on the screen.
5. **Save your backup codes.** On the same 2-Step Verification page, choose **Backup codes**, then **Get backup codes**. You get 10 codes; each has 8 digits and works only once. You need them if you lose your phone. Print them and keep them with your important papers, such as your passport.

### Apple Account (iPhone)

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap your name, then **Sign-In & Security**.
3. Turn on **Two-Factor Authentication** and follow the instructions on the screen.

If it is already on, this step is done. Apple says it is the default security method for most accounts.

> The verification code is for you alone. Never give it to anyone, even if they say they are from your bank or from Google. Do not give your backup codes to anyone either: Google only asks for them on the sign-in screen.

## Try it

Open your Google or Apple account settings now, and answer these two questions:

- Is two-step verification on? If not, turn it on with the steps above.
- If you have a Google Account: have you saved your backup codes somewhere safe?

If you want to go further: open the security settings of another important account, such as your email or bank, and look for "two-step verification" or "two-factor authentication". If it offers you backup codes, save those too.

## Key points

- Two-step verification adds a second lock: your password, then a confirmation that usually comes to your phone.
- Turn it on for your Google or Apple account, then for your other important accounts, such as email and bank.
- Never give your verification code or backup codes to anyone, and keep your backup codes somewhere safe.
