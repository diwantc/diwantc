## Why it matters

Every day you get messages on your phone and in your email. Some are scams that pretend to come from a bank, a delivery company or a government office, to take your data or your money. In this lesson you learn signs that give them away, before you tap anything in them.

## The idea

A scammer pretends to be an organization or a person you trust, then rushes you to act before you think. This trick is called phishing. It can reach you by text message (SMS), WhatsApp message or email.

A scam message has six signs:

1. **You were not expecting it:** it comes from a number you don't know, or it is about a prize in a contest you never entered.
2. **It claims to come from someone you trust:** a bank, a government office, a delivery company, or someone you know.
3. **It rushes or scares you:** "within 24 hours", "your account will be suspended".
4. **It promises a prize or money waiting for you.**
5. **It asks for your details or money:** to update your details, send your card number, your PIN or a verification code, or pay a fee.
6. **It has a link to tap, or a number to call.** A link is a word or an address you tap to open a page.

Do not relax because a sign is missing. A scammer can fake the sender's number, and the message can even appear among your bank's real messages. It can come from a friend's stolen account, or while you really are expecting a parcel. It may have spelling mistakes, but some scams are well written.

The next two lessons show why you never give anyone a verification code (the number you get to sign in to your account), and how to check a link before you open it.

## Step by step

We wrote these messages as examples of scam types that official bodies in Saudi Arabia and elsewhere have warned about. The amounts are made up.

### Example 1: a parcel waiting for payment (text message)

> **From:** a number you don't know
>
> Your parcel has arrived but we could not deliver it. Pay a 12-riyal delivery fee within 24 hours or the parcel will be returned. Pay here: (link)

**The signs:** a number you don't know, a short deadline, and a fee to pay through a link.

### Example 2: "Update your details" (text message)

> **From:** a number you don't know
>
> Dear customer, we have temporarily blocked your card. To reactivate it, update your details using the link, or call the number below.

**The signs:** it claims to be your bank, scares you with a blocked card, and asks you to update your details through a link or a number in the message.

### Example 3: a prize in a contest you never entered (WhatsApp)

> **From:** a number you don't know
>
> Congratulations! Your number has won a prize worth 50,000 riyals. The offer ends today. To claim it, send your full name and your bank card number.

**The signs:** a prize in a contest you never entered, a short deadline, and a request for bank details.

### What to do if you are unsure about a message

1. Do not tap a link in it, do not reply, and do not call the number written in it.
2. Check for yourself: open the organization's official app, or call the number on its official website or on your card.
3. If the message claims to be from someone you know, call them by voice or video on the number you already had saved.
4. Never give anyone your PIN or a verification code, even if they say they are from the bank. And do not give your details to anyone until you have checked who they are.

## Try it

Open your text messages, WhatsApp and email, and look for a message that asks you to pay, update your details or tap a link. How many of the six signs does it have?

A sign does not always mean a scam, but it is reason enough to check for yourself before you tap anything. If you cannot find a message like this, apply the signs to the three examples.

## Key points

- A scam message pretends to come from an organization or a person you trust, and rushes you to act before you think.
- Its signs: it is unexpected, it scares you or promises a prize, it asks for your details or money, or it has a link or a number to call.
- If you are unsure: do not tap and do not reply. Check for yourself in the organization's official app or with its number.
