## Why it matters

Someone calls and says they are from your bank: "There is a problem with your account. We sent you a code; read it to me so we can fix it." Or you get a WhatsApp message from a friend's number: "I sent you a code by mistake, send it to me." In both cases the scammer wants your verification code. In this lesson you learn why you never give it to anyone, and you protect your WhatsApp account.

## The idea

A verification code is a number you get by text message or email when you sign in to your account. It is also called a one-time passcode (OTP). Your password and the code work together, so someone who knows only one of them cannot get in.

The scammer may already know your password or some of your details, so the code is all they are missing. That is why they try to make you read it to them. Someone who gets your code may get into your account, take your money or data, and pretend to be you.

The rule is simple: **the code is for you alone.** Anyone who asks you for it is a scammer, even if they say they are from the bank or an official body, and even if the request comes from a friend's or relative's number.

On WhatsApp you get a registration code, 6 digits, by text message or call when your number is registered on a phone. Someone who gets it may take your account, then message your friends and groups as you.

## Step by step

### If someone asks you for the code

1. Do not read it out or send it. End the call, or stop replying.
2. If you are worried about your account, call the bank yourself on a number you trust, such as the one on your bank statement or in the bank's app. Do not call the number the caller gave you, and do not trust the bank's name on your screen: a scammer can make a call or a message look like it comes from the bank.
3. If the request came from a friend's number, call your friend yourself on the number you already had saved, to check.

### Turn on two-step verification in WhatsApp

Two-step verification protects your account with two things together: the code you receive, and a password you choose. That makes it harder for someone who took your code to register your number on another phone. But it is extra protection: do not give the code to anyone even after you turn it on.

**Android:**

1. Open WhatsApp, tap the three dots ⋮, then Settings > Account > Two-step verification.
2. Switch on Turn on two-step verification.
3. Create a strong password you will remember, at least 8 characters with at least one letter and one number, then enter it again to confirm.
4. Enter an email address you can open and tap Next, or tap Skip.
5. Tap Trust this device or Skip.

**iPhone:**

1. Open WhatsApp, tap You, then Account > Two-step verification.
2. Follow steps 2 to 5 as on Android.

If you forget the password when signing in, you can verify another way, for example with a code sent to your email or by text message. If you cannot, you may have to wait a few days to reset your account, and some of your messages may be deleted.

WhatsApp is replacing the PIN, a secret 6-digit number, with a password. If you see PIN instead of password, follow what your screen shows.

> Your two-step verification password is like the verification code: do not give it to anyone.

## Try it

Open WhatsApp now and turn on two-step verification with the steps above. If it is already on and you do not remember the password, create a new one while you are signed in: you do not need the old one.

## Key points

- The verification code is for you alone. Anyone who asks for it is a scammer, even if they say they are from the bank.
- Someone who gets your code may get into your account, take your money or data, and pretend to be you.
- Turn on two-step verification in WhatsApp, choose a password you will remember, and do not give it to anyone.
