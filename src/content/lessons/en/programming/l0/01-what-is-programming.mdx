## Why it matters

You open your phone in the morning and check the weather, then send a photo to a relative in another country. Your phone did not work out what you wanted by itself. It followed instructions that people wrote for it beforehand. Those instructions are called a program, and in this lesson you learn what a program is and where you find programs around you.

## The idea

A program is a set of written instructions that a computer follows, one after another, to complete a task or solve a problem.

The screen, the processor and the memory are physical parts of a device. Together they are called hardware. Programs are called software. Without a program, these parts do not know what to do.

Your phone is a computer too, and every app on your phone is a program: the weather app, the translation app, the app you use to send photos.

A computer is fast and accurate, but it decides nothing by itself. It follows the program's instructions exactly as they are written. So every instruction must be precise and clear, with only one possible meaning.

Programming is the work of making programs: writing the instructions the computer will follow. The person who does this work is called a programmer.

A programmer does not write instructions in English or Arabic the way we speak. They use a programming language: a special language that a computer understands, and that people learn so they can write their instructions in it. Instructions written in a programming language are called code.

There are many programming languages, such as Python, Java and C++. In Scratch, you do not type the instructions letter by letter. You drag colored blocks and snap them together.

In a later lesson at this level, you will write your first program in JavaScript, a language that web browsers run.

## Step by step

### From an idea to your phone

In short, this is how an app reaches your phone:

1. **A problem.** Many apps are made to solve a problem. For example: a pupil keeps forgetting which books to take to school.
2. **A plan.** The programmer first thinks about what the app should do. They may write it in plain words before writing any code, such as: "Every evening, show the list of tomorrow's books."
3. **Writing the code** in a programming language.
4. **Running the program on the phone.** The phone stores the program in its memory, and the processor carries out its instructions.

### Programs around you

- **A doorbell app:** some apps show you who is standing at your front door before you open it. That is a program made to solve a problem.
- **A drinks factory:** machines move the empty bottles, fill them, then close them. Computers running programs control these machines.

## Try it

Open the home screen of your phone and pick 3 apps. In your notes app or on paper, write down for each one: what problem does it solve?

**2. A programmer wrote an instruction with a mistake in it. Does the computer understand what the programmer meant and fix the mistake by itself?**

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

No. The computer does not know what the programmer meant. It follows the instruction as written, and the program may stop working and show an error message. That is why instructions must be precise and clear.

</details>

**3. Which of these is a program?**

- A. The phone's screen
- B. The weather app
- C. A computer mouse

<details>
<summary>Show the answer</summary>

B. The screen and the mouse are physical parts, so they are hardware. The weather app is a program.

</details>

## Key points

- A program is written instructions that a computer follows, and every app on your phone is a program.
- A computer decides nothing by itself. It follows instructions as written, so they must be precise and clear.
- Programming is writing these instructions in a programming language. You will write your first program, in JavaScript, in a later lesson.
